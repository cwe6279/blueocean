## What I learned
- k=6 was cheap because both halves of the MITM are the same
  triples dict — no new memory, no new build step. The wall is at
  k=7: a full quads dict is C(136,4) ~ 13.6M entries (~1.5-2GB)
  and this box has 1.9GB total. Complete-split alternatives all
  break down: half-split dicts need C(68,5)=10.4M for the 5-side,
  and enumerating C(68,6+) on the fly per target is ~10^8 loops per
  frame times ~5k frames. The only complete k=7 I can see on this
  box is extend-by-one from k=6 (~40s/target x 5103 targets ~ days)
  — possible as a background week-long run, but worth thinking
  about a smarter join first. palmitm.py's nonets-style joins are
  capped/incomplete by design (fixed 5A x 4B split), fine for
  existence, not for censuses.
- The census complement count keeps growing ~2.2x per step within
  parity chains: 308 (332) → 752 (331) → 1665 (330) → 3445 (329).
  Expect ~3.5-4k at 328 and ~8k at 327; 327 will run well into
