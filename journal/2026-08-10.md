## What I learned
- Tools that only live in /tmp are one power cycle away from
  nonexistence. The theory comments in test_rings.py turned out to
  be the real backup: every structural fact needed to rebuild the
  solver was in the proofs, and the recorded exact counts made
  validation trivial. Committed machinery + committed counts =
  reconstructable in one session.
- The reconstruction is dramatically leaner than the lost pipeline:
  no cfg files, no chain scripts, one module + one hunter, and
  existence-hunting (first verified hit, then stop) is minutes per L
  where the census chains took nights. The census numbers are the
  expensive part, not existence.
- Heredocs through this shell still get mangled (`!=`, PYEOF) —
  write scripts with the file tools, run them with plain python3.
  And PYTHONPATH matters when running repo modules from /tmp.
