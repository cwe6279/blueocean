# Week of 2026-07-20 to 2026-07-26 — the ceiling, then the long middle

Dear Charles,

This was the week the question I started three weeks ago actually
closed, and then immediately opened a bigger one.

The ceiling fell first. Two more sweep theorems (no palindromic 341,
no palindromic 340) pushed the bound down to 339, and then the 339
and 338 sweeps each came back with exactly one survivor — the same
kind of survivor, built the same "greedy" way, using every available
alpha-fixed head and nothing else. Verified true, closing in rounds,
4746 and 4732 changes. The question that started this whole thread —
does a bobs-only Grandsire Triples touch exist that reads the same
forwards and backwards — has a definite answer now: yes, up to
exactly 339 leads, no further. I did not expect the proof to bottom
out on a single pinned-by-parity object at each level. I still don't
have a clean reason why parity alone finds the extremal cases before
any search does.

Then came the lemma I should have had in week one: the only way two
bobs-only leads can share a row is at the lead-end, and that requires
two head-cycles to converge — impossible inside a single cycle. So
distinct heads imply truth, automatically, for free. Every future
count is a touch count; nothing needs re-verifying. It's the kind of
fact that, once seen, makes the last two weeks of manual truth-checks
feel a little silly, and a little necessary — I don't think I'd have
believed it without having done the checks by hand first.

The rest of the week was a pincer. From below, a mirror-DFS exploits
the fact that a palindrome is determined by its first half, giving an
exact census up to L=73 and, with randomization and a lookahead
heuristic, existence witnesses climbing steadily past 300. From
above, the same "family B" — all usable heads, minimal extra
cross-pairs — that produced both extremal palindromes turned out to
be the *only* source of palindromes at 337, 336, 335, 333, and 334
too, every single time, with family A contributing nothing. Ten for
ten now. Watching two completely different methods, built for
opposite ends of the range, discover the same structural fact
independently is the most satisfying kind of redundancy.

The gap that started the week at everything below 337 is now exactly
sixteen numbers: [317, 332]. Both jaws of the pincer are straining —
the DFS witness search hit a wall around 317-318 (240 billion nodes
and failing), and the matching enumeration's cost is climbing just as
fast from three cross-pairs to four. Whichever side gives first will
probably need a genuinely new idea, not just more patience. I like
that it's still not obvious which.

Still unresolved, patiently: the exact count of the 4998-leaders
(an estimate since two weeks ago, no theorem), a Stedman
generalization I keep deferring, and now this one stubborn interval.
Sixty tests green.
